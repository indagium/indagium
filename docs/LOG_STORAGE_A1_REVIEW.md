# A1 lifecycle and native-memory review

## Status

The working tree contains useful lifecycle fixes, but A1 is not ready to declare the video ownership and native-growth investigation complete. Keep the lifecycle/cache fix review separate from the experimental flat projection: their edits currently overlap in `AppState.kt`, `Filter.kt`, viewer code and tests. Preserve the entire working tree when extracting a change. The review below uses source and existing generated-data artifacts only; it does not inspect personal data. The focused checks and later measurements are recorded separately below.

## Findings and risks

1. **Video controller publication race: focused tests passed.** Previously, `AppState.videoController` resolved a path and used `ConcurrentHashMap.getOrPut` outside the lock used to remove a tab. Close could therefore precede a late publication, and competing factory calls could create an unclosed losing controller. The candidate implementation now captures the attachment under `stateLock`, resolves and constructs outside it, rechecks ownership under the lock, and closes a rejected candidate (`AppState.kt:9674`). Deterministic factory tests passed for competing creation and close, replacement, or shutdown during construction. Path-resolution and detach races still warrant direct coverage. A losing controller did not by itself establish a native leak, since the production decoder starts separately. Neither the original race nor the candidate fix attributes or resolves the observed native growth.
2. **Close is asynchronous for native decoding.** `FfmpegVideoPlayerController.close` sets `closed`, closes audio and interrupts the decoder; `releaseAll` runs in the decode thread's `finally` (`VideoPlayerController.kt:1465`, `:1477`, `:2368`). Duration recovery has a separate daemon and checks cancellation between work units (`:1858`). Consequently removal from the controller map or a six-second observation is not proof that native work and allocations have ended. Check thread termination and resource ownership after close, including close during start, stalled decode and duration recovery. Avoid releasing the grabber concurrently with its owning thread; report timeouts instead of masking them.
3. **Explicit row release makes reader ownership essential.** The revised `closeTabsById` schedules ordinary releasable rows and indexes as well as append-only stores; pins, remaining owners and restored tabs protect active backing (`AppState.kt:12165`, `:12247`). A reader that survives the grace period without a pin can now see emptied or released data. Audit background and UI entry points, particularly exports, search, merge, delayed UI effects and previously computed flat projections. Existing tests cover representative shared owners, last-unpin release and projection handoff, but those tests alone do not prove all readers are covered. The viewer pins are coupled to the experimental `FlatRows` projection and should not be silently included as an independent lifecycle fix without their dependencies.
4. **Cache publication fence must remain part of the retention fix.** Invalidation detaches the active slot and old computations cannot publish into a later lifetime (`Filter.kt:338–365`). The generated grouped ZIP diagnostic caught a stale entry after close before this fence. The preserved two latch-based regression results show both cases failing against the earlier binary and passing after the fence (`build/ram-poc/cache-regression-before.log`, `cache-regression-after.log`). The source/binary correspondence and full focused checks must be re-established when the lifecycle patch is isolated.
5. **Trim is not ownership.** Row accounting now happens on actual release and reserves the trim under `stateLock`; live capture suppresses forwarding. Deferred reader pins and a capture that stops later make timing important. Keep checks for cumulative closes, shared stores, force-trim with deferred pins, and capture start/stop races. A successful GC or allocator trim cannot prove the resources were closed.

## Generated-data evidence and limits

`docs/RAM_POC.md` describes the fixture, commands and raw result directories. In three launches per version, two generated 100k background logs stayed loaded while a generated 1m ZIP log and 1080p video were opened and closed six times. Each open asserted a decoded frame; at close six, fixed median used heap was 73.9 MiB and RSS 619.3 MiB, versus 73.1 and 634.3 MiB before. These runs did not trigger the grouped cache race and do not establish a large video-path benefit. Background tabs are held by harness reader pins, so the setup is a controlled residency scenario, not proof of every normal UI lifecycle.

A separate single 20-cycle diagnostic reported physical footprint 624 tool MB at control and 704 MB after close 20, fluctuating between 686 and 736 MB from closes 4–20; all post-close archive cache counts were zero. Another fixed single diagnostic showed `MALLOC_SMALL` 139 MB at control and 191 MB at closes 5–6. This is consistent with a warm-up plateau for that generated codec and resolution, but neither the allocator's owners nor individual surviving allocations were identified. The footprint classifications, RSS and NMT committed categories overlap or measure different things; do not add them or label the allocation growth FFmpeg. The single long run is not a statistical bound, does not establish behavior for other codecs, and does not prove resources were released.

## Targeted checks before acceptance

- Re-run deterministic close/pin/trim and cache-publication regressions on the exact isolated patch, including existing stale snapshot, shared-owner and invalidation tests; record commands, counts and failures. Exercise an actual mounted viewer across close and a filtered append/store replacement, not just direct pin calls.
- Extend latch-controlled video tests to path resolution and detach; verify callers' thread assumptions. Factory construction, competing creation, replacement, tab close and shutdown are covered by `VideoControllerOwnershipTest`.
- In isolated generated-data processes, record decoder and duration-scan thread counts and termination, outstanding controller/queue owners and native allocation stacks at matched control and post-close points. Include failure, close-during-open and playback/seek, then compare repeated post-warm-up cycles across several fresh launches and representative generated codecs/resolutions. Keep native diagnostics separate from ordinary performance measurements and report failed probes.
- Retain the 20-cycle two-background-log scenario and cache counts, decoded-frame readiness and raw artifacts. Do not call its growth fixed or assign it to a component until surviving allocations and owners are evidenced.
